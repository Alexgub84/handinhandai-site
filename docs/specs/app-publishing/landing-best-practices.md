# Information-first landing pages: pattern, evidence, and a section flow for /app-publishing

Research date: 2 Nov 2026. Source offer doc: `docs/specs/app-publishing/offer.md` ("Final website package (v2)" and "Market research" sections). Homepage about-content read from `src/components/home/About.astro` and `src/data/home-profile.ts`.

## Summary

The pattern is most precisely called an **advertorial / content-led landing page** (reads like editorial content, links to the offer woven in, sells only once trust is built), a close cousin of the **pre-sell page** (same shape, named for its funnel position) and, when it is long and handles objections before price, a **long-form sales page**. The strongest evidence for the shape is Backlinko's own documented test — embedding a call-to-action inside long article content lifted conversions from 0.54% to 4.82%, a 785% increase ([backlinko.com](https://backlinko.com/increase-conversions)) — plus Conversion Rate Experts' agency case study for Moz, where a page six times longer than the control lifted sales 52% on 5,000+ visitors ([conversion-rate-experts.com](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/moz-case-study/)). CXL's own position is conditional, not universal: long-form wins specifically when the offer is complex, carries real price, and the visitor doesn't already trust the seller ([cxl.com](https://cxl.com/blog/long-form-or-short-form/)) — which matches this offer (a stranger holding your Apple/Google account access) better than almost any other service Alex sells. Recommendation: build the page as an advertorial that answers the real questions first, threads soft links to the service between sections, and shows the price table only at the end, exactly as the owner specified.

## What the pattern is and where it is documented

- **Advertorial / content-led landing page**: "reads like editorial content... built to warm up cold traffic before asking for anything," blending persuasive intent with editorial structure so the reader is in content-consumption mode when the first soft link appears ([heyflow.com](https://heyflow.com/blog/how-to-create-advertorial-landing-page/), [adlibrary.com](https://adlibrary.com/posts/advertorial-landing-pages)).
- **Pre-sell / presell page**: names the same shape by its job in the funnel ("sits between your ad and your offer... its job is not to sell [outright]") rather than by its editorial voice ([getlandra.com](https://www.getlandra.com/glossary/what-is-a-presell-page)). "Advertorial" and "presell" describe the same page from two angles — style vs. funnel position.
- **Long-form sales page**: Unbounce's own canonical structure for this format is a narrative, not a list of boxes: set up the problem → establish your qualifications → show how the solution fixes the problem → list benefits → answer objections → describe the result → state exactly what the buyer receives ([unbounce.com](https://unbounce.com/landing-pages/long-form-sales-pages/)). This is the backbone the section flow below follows, just with the article questions doing the "problem" and "objections" work instead of sales copy.
- **When long-form beats short-form (and vice versa), per CXL**: a gym membership page converted better short; an insulation company's page (complex, higher price) converted better long. The deciding factors are offer complexity, price, and how much the visitor already trusts the seller — not length for its own sake ([cxl.com/blog/long-form-or-short-form](https://cxl.com/blog/long-form-or-short-form/)).
- **Julian Shapiro's startup landing-page structure** is the mainstream *short* counter-pattern (navbar → hero → social proof → CTA → features/objections → repeat CTA → footer) ([julian.com](https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages), per search synthesis — the full handbook text was not independently fetched). It is the pattern Alex's page should deliberately not copy, because it front-loads the single CTA instead of the information.
- **Google's own stance (helpful-content guidance)** does not name a landing-page pattern, but its self-assessment questions reward exactly this shape: "does your content clearly demonstrate first-hand expertise," "will someone leave feeling they've learned enough to achieve their goal," and it warns against content "made primarily to gain search engine rankings" ([developers.google.com/.../creating-helpful-content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), fetched 2 Nov 2026). An information-first page that genuinely explains the stores is the compliant version of SEO content; a page stuffed with keyword-shaped headings and no real answers is the violation.

## Section flow for /app-publishing

All copy content below is drawn from `offer.md`'s "Final website package (v2)" section unless noted. Order runs top to bottom.

1. **Opening / H1** — Job: answer the visitor's real question in the first screen instead of pitching (NN/g: 79% of visitors scan, not read, so the answer must be visible before any CTA — [nngroup.com](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/)). Content: keep the existing H1 ("האפליקציה שלכם בחנויות תוך ימים. בלי 12 טסטרים, בלי 14 יום המתנה, בלי מק.") and sub-line from offer.md, but frame it as the lead paragraph of an article, not a banner — state the 12-testers/14-day fact and the Mac/Xcode fact as plain claims, not hype. CTA: **soft text link only** ("או דלגו על ההסבר ושלחו לבדיקת מוכנות") pointing down to section 5's WhatsApp button — no button here. Image: a small byline strip, Alex's existing portrait crop at thumbnail size next to "5 שנים בתחום," establishing a named author per Google's first-hand-expertise question, not a full hero photo.
2. **"מה בודקים בפרסום אפליקציה בגוגל פליי"** — Job: explain Google's actual gate so the visitor understands why it hurts them. Content: the 12-testers/14-days closed-testing rule on new personal developer accounts since 13 Nov 2023, what resets the counter (a tester uninstalling), and that verified org accounts with a D-U-N-S are exempt (offer.md, "Platform rules and risks"). CTA: one soft inline link — "חשבון עסקי מאומת פוטר מהדרישה הזו, וזה מה שאנחנו מריצים עליו" — linking to section 9 (pricing) or section 5 (what we do), not a button. Image: a real screenshot of the Play Console closed-testing / 12-tester progress screen (own account, de-identified) — this is the single most concrete, believable image the page can carry, because it is proof the writer has done this, not an illustration of it.
3. **"למה אפל מחייבת חשבון משלכם"** — Job: explain Apple's rule before the visitor can object "why can't you just publish it for me like Google." Content: Apple's transfer criteria, Guideline 5.2.1 (app must be submitted by the IP owner) and 4.2.6 (agencies publishing many client apps under one account get rejected) (offer.md, "Platform rules and risks"). CTA: soft inline link to section 5's explanation of how the Admin-invite model works. Image: own screenshot of the App Store Connect "Users and Access → Invite" screen, the exact step the client has to perform — reused from the client-instructions content already written in offer.md Page 3.
4. **"איך נראית דחייה, ומה עושים איתה"** — Job: defuse the scariest unknown (rejection) before asking for trust or money. Content: that first-submission rejection on Apple is common, what the emails look like ("We noticed an issue," "Your submission was accepted"), and that the service's job is translating the rejection into plain Hebrew and resubmitting (offer.md Page 3, "מיילים שתקבלו מאפל"). CTA: one soft inline link down to the FAQ's "מה אם האפליקציה נדחית?" entry. Image: an annotated diagram of a real (own, anonymized) rejection email with callouts on the parts that matter — a diagram, not a screenshot, since it needs explanatory arrows.
5. **"מה אנחנו עושים בפועל"** — Job: the pivot point from pure information to the offer, still told as process, not pitch. Content: the 5-step flow already drafted in offer.md ("מה אנחנו עושים" + "איך זה עובד, צעד אחר צעד"): readiness check (free, before payment) → Google publish from the verified org account → Apple publish via Admin invite → updates → Google transfer. CTA: **first real CTA, a WhatsApp button** — "שלחו את האפליקציה לבדיקת מוכנות חינם" — justified precisely because it asks for a free check, not money, so it can appear this early without breaking the "objections before price" rule. Image: one annotated process diagram (own SVG, 5 icons: send → check → Google → Apple → updates/transfer), mirroring the icon structure already specified in offer.md's v1 "How it works."
6. **"למי זה מתאים, ולמי לא"** — Job: self-selection, not persuasion. Content: offer.md's "למי זה מתאים" (AI-built app stuck on testers, no Mac, business that doesn't want to hire a developer, startup needing an MVP live this week) and "מה זה לא" (banned categories, no bug fixes, no approval guarantee). CTA: **none** — this section's job is to filter, and a CTA here would undercut the honesty of "who it's not for." Image: none, or a small non-photographic icon row (not a focal image).
7. **"הבעלות שלכם, שחור על גבי לבן"** — Job: the trust/ownership block, placed right before the about-the-person section so the two reinforce each other. Content: offer.md's "הבעלות שלכם" (app/code/brand stays the client's, signed agreement before any upload, no password sharing, Google transfer is an official Google process, Apple app lives on the client's account from day one). CTA: soft link to the sample agreement PDF ("הסכם לדוגמה"). Image: none required.
8. **"מי עומד מאחורי השירות"** — Job: the about-Alex block the owner asked for. Content, adapted from the homepage's existing voice (`src/components/home/About.astro`, `src/data/home-profile.ts`): first-person Hebrew, the "5 שנים בתחום" counter, the identity framing ("אבא של רון," "יזם," "איש של אנשים וטכנולוגיה"), and the core trust line already written there — "לא מקבלים קופסה שחורה של קוד, אלא בנאדם שאפשר לסמוך עליו, לדבר איתו בגובה העיניים." Re-angle one sentence specifically to app publishing: why a person who builds AI automations also personally walks each app through Apple and Google review, instead of routing it through an anonymous queue. CTA: the same personal WhatsApp link already used on the homepage ("בואו נדבר, בגובה העיניים") — soft, not transactional. Image: **reuse the existing portrait** `/home/alex-about.webp` — the brief already notes this image exists; do not shoot a new one.
9. **"מחירים"** — Job: the price table, deliberately last among substantive sections, once every objection (testers, Mac, rejection, ownership, who's behind it) has already been answered. Content: offer.md's final v2 pricing table (Google launch 590 ₪, Apple launch 790 ₪, both stores 1,290 ₪, monthly update/hosting plans, transfer 290 ₪, extra update 120 ₪/store, screenshots add-on), plus the "not included" line and the VAT/payment-after-check note. CTA: a WhatsApp button, same copy as section 5 ("שלחו לבדיקת מוכנות") — not a "buy" button, because offer.md's own model is payment only after the free readiness check approves the app. Image: none, or small Google Play / App Store wordmark badges as supporting icons — never the focal element of a pricing section.
10. **"שאלות נפוצות"** — Job: mop up remaining objections in their own words, after price, which is where FAQ conventionally sits and where it stays here. Content: the nine Q&As already drafted in offer.md (why Google/Apple split, do I need a Mac, when can I transfer on Google, what if rejected, what's checked in the readiness check, do you fix bugs, how long it takes, what to send, what's banned, multi-app discount). CTA: the "האם אני צריך מק?" answer links out to the separate article, exactly as offer.md's SEO plan specifies ("landing page links to the article from the 'צריך מק?' FAQ"). No other CTA inside the FAQ.
11. **Final CTA band** — Job: the one hard ask on the page, repeated once at the very end. Content: offer.md's existing closing line ("שלחו את האפליקציה לבדיקת מוכנות חינם... תוך יום עסקים תקבלו תשובה... התשלום רק אחרי האישור שלנו"). CTA: WhatsApp button, same destination as sections 5 and 9. Image: none.
12. **Footer** — Job: legal/contact plus the link back to the main site. Content: Hand In Hand AI, עוסק פטור 323227199, email, WhatsApp, privacy policy, terms, sample agreement — and a link back to `/` (the homepage), satisfying the "link back to the main site" requirement. Image: none.

## CTA strategy

- **Count and weight**: roughly nine CTA touchpoints — six soft inline text links (sections 1–4, 7, 10) threaded between informational paragraphs, and three WhatsApp buttons that all point to the same action (sections 5, 9, 11). This matches the shape Backlinko measured directly: two links embedded inside long article content, not one end-of-page button, captured 30% of all new subscribers against 54 other opt-in forms on the site ([backlinko.com/increase-conversions](https://backlinko.com/increase-conversions), confirmed by direct fetch). A specific "4 to 6 CTAs is normal for a long-form page" rule of thumb appears in practitioner guides (e.g. [canvasbuilder.co/blog/long-form-sales-page-structure](https://canvasbuilder.co/blog/long-form-sales-page-structure)) but is not an independently verified test — treat it as a sanity check, not evidence, which is why the design above leans on the Backlinko number instead.
- **Escalation**: every soft link in sections 1–4 points down the page rather than out to WhatsApp — it moves the reader, it doesn't ask for anything. The first WhatsApp button (section 5) only appears once the mechanism (free readiness check, paid only after approval) has been explained, so clicking it never feels like a purchase. Sections 9 and 11 repeat the identical button and copy rather than escalating further, because offer.md's own model has no harder ask than "send it for a free check" — there is no separate "buy now" moment on this page by design.
- **Why price sits after objections, not CTAs**: Unbounce's long-form narrative order (problem → qualifications → solution → benefits → objections → result → what you get) puts price-adjacent content last ([unbounce.com/landing-pages/long-form-sales-pages](https://unbounce.com/landing-pages/long-form-sales-pages/)); CXL's conditional case for long-form specifically cites unresolved trust and complexity as the reason to defer the ask ([cxl.com/blog/long-form-or-short-form](https://cxl.com/blog/long-form-or-short-form/)). Both support putting the ownership/trust block and the about-Alex section immediately before pricing (sections 7–8), so the price lands right after trust is established, which is this page's riskiest trust gap (a stranger holding store-account access).

No stock photography and no decorative illustration anywhere on the page. Evidence: a CXL review of Nielsen's eye-tracking work and a MarketingExperiments test found visitors largely ignore stock "people" photos while a real photo of an actual customer lifted sign-ups 35% against the client's best-performing stock image ([cxl.com/blog/stock-photography-vs-real-photos-cant-use](https://cxl.com/blog/stock-photography-vs-real-photos-cant-use/) — read via search synthesis, not independently fetched, so treat the 35% figure as reported-by-CXL rather than independently confirmed here). For a one-person, done-for-you technical service, every image on the page should be something only this operator could have produced (own console screens, own past rejection email, own portrait) — a generic "person at laptop" stock photo would actively read as the opposite of the trust the page is trying to build.

**Accessibility**: every screenshot and diagram needs real alt text describing what it shows and why it matters (e.g. "מסך קונסולת גוגל פליי המציג את דרישת 12 הטסטרים למשך 14 יום"), per WCAG 1.1.1 — informative images get a real description, purely decorative elements (if any) get `alt=""` so screen readers skip them ([w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/non-text-content](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/non-text-content.html), fetched 2 Nov 2026).

**Page weight**: no image on this page should be the Largest Contentful Paint element if it can be avoided — the byline thumbnail (image 1) is small enough not to compete, and nothing else sits above the fold as a dominant image. Practitioner guidance (not an official Google number) suggests keeping any hero-weight image under ~200KB and converting to WebP, which typically removes 70–90% of a raw JPEG's weight ([mochify.app/guides/optimizing-hero-images](https://mochify.app/guides/optimizing-hero-images) — this is a third-party recommendation, not a Google-published budget). Lazy-load every image below the fold (all of 2–7 above); never lazy-load whatever ends up being the actual LCP element.

## Length and tone

Target: roughly 1,100–1,600 Hebrew words across sections 1–9 (excluding FAQ), with the FAQ adding another ~500–700 words — long enough to carry the full objection-handling arc, deliberately short of the separate article's ~1,800-word depth (`offer.md`'s SEO plan already reserves that depth for the linked article, not this page). The case for this length: CXL's own framing is conditional, not absolute — long-form wins specifically for complex, higher-price offers where the visitor doesn't already trust the seller ([cxl.com/blog/long-form-or-short-form](https://cxl.com/blog/long-form-or-short-form/)), which is this offer exactly (a stranger with Apple/Google account access, real shekels, zero existing relationship). Conversion Rate Experts' Moz page — six times longer than its control — lifted sales 52% on a 5,000+ visitor sample ([conversion-rate-experts.com/moz-case-study](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/moz-case-study/)), the closest primary-source precedent for "longer wins" at this kind of complexity. Against this, note CXL also reports a case where a gym membership page (simple, low price, high familiarity) converted *better* short — length is not free, it is a bet that only pays off when complexity and price are real, which they are here.

Tone: Nielsen Norman Group's own usability tests found that promotional language imposes a measurable cognitive cost — readers have to work to filter hyperbole from fact — while concise, scannable, objective writing improved measured task performance by up to 124% combined ([nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/), fetched 2 Nov 2026, 79%/16% scan-vs-read figures and the 124% combined-improvement figure both confirmed in that fetch). Keep the existing homepage register — direct, first person, "בגובה העיניים" — rather than switching to a more formal or sales-coded voice for this page.

**Hebrew/RTL specifics**: no primary UX research (NN/g, CXL, or equivalent) covering Hebrew/RTL long-form pages specifically was found — this is a real gap, say so rather than invent one. What exists is marketing-trade-blog opinion, not evidence: Israeli consumers reportedly treat WhatsApp as a personal rather than corporate channel and respond to Hebrew-language, Israeli-phone-number trust signals over a generic contact form ([waliner.io/click-to-whatsapp-ads-in-israel](https://waliner.io/click-to-whatsapp-ads-in-israel/), [niv-t.co.il/en/blog/israeli-market-website-localization](https://www.niv-t.co.il/en/blog/israeli-market-website-localization/)). This matches the WhatsApp-as-CTA-channel choice already made for the homepage and offer.md, but it should be read as reasonable local convention, not tested fact.

## SEO and structured data

- **H1**: already correct in offer.md — keep the main commercial phrase ("פרסום אפליקציה בגוגל פליי ובאפ סטור," per offer.md's own SEO plan table) inside the H1 rather than only in the title tag.
- **H2s as literal visitor questions**: sections 2–4 above should use the exact phrases offer.md's SEO plan already identified as how people actually search — "12 טסטרים גוגל פליי," "האם צריך מק כדי להעלות אפליקציה," "חשבון מפתח גוגל פליי עסקי / D-U-N-S" — as real H2 headings, not paraphrased. This is already decided in `offer.md`'s own SEO plan table; this document just confirms it fits the information-first structure.
- **Structured data**: mark the page's primary entity as **Service** (schema.org), not Article — Google's own Article documentation expects a news/blog-shaped primary purpose (`headline`, `author`, `datePublished` as the main content) ([developers.google.com/.../structured-data/article](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article), fetched 2 Nov 2026), which this page is not; it is a commercial service page that happens to read like an article. Reserve Article schema for the separate linked article page instead. Add **FAQPage** as a secondary type on the same page for the FAQ block, and **Person**/**Organization** for the about-Alex section, combined in one JSON-LD block via `@graph`. The specific caution against declaring two *competing* primary entities on one URL (e.g. both Article and Service as if each were the main thing) is practitioner consensus, not an explicit Google rule — found in secondary SEO sources, not Google's own documentation ([vibecodeyourleads.com/pillar-3/cluster-3e/node-1](https://www.vibecodeyourleads.com/pillar-3/cluster-3e/node-1), [incremys.com/en/resources/blog/schema-seo](https://www.incremys.com/en/resources/blog/schema-seo)) — treated here as a reasonable precaution, not proven fact.
- **FAQPage caveat**: keep the FAQPage markup for semantic clarity and for AI answer engines, but do not expect a visible rich snippet from it in Google Search results — multiple 2026 SEO trade sources report Google retired the FAQ rich result sometime in 2026 ([seoscore.tools/blog/faq-schema-markup](https://seoscore.tools/blog/faq-schema-markup/), [quattr.com/blog/faq-schema-in-2026](https://www.quattr.com/blog/faq-schema-in-2026/), [elementera.com/blog/google-has-removed-faq-rich-results](https://www.elementera.com/blog/google-has-removed-faq-rich-results)). The exact retirement date is reported inconsistently across these sources (some say 7 May 2026, one says January 2026) — treat the fact of retirement as corroborated and the specific date as unconfirmed.
- **Helpful-content fit**: Google's own self-assessment questions ("does the content demonstrate first-hand expertise," "will someone leave feeling they learned enough") are best satisfied by the real screenshots and the readiness-check specifics in sections 2–5, not by generic explainer prose — this is the direct link between the imagery plan above and the SEO plan ([developers.google.com/.../creating-helpful-content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).

1. **[median.co/blog/publish-vibe-coded-app-to-app-store-google-play](https://median.co/blog/publish-vibe-coded-app-to-app-store-google-play)** — same niche as this page (publishing vibe-coded apps to both stores). Section order: "App store submission starts with a stable web app URL" → "A wrapper is not enough for store submission" → per-platform sections (Lovable, Base44, Emergent, Google AI Studio, Replit, Vercel/Netlify) each with a soft link to that platform's own conversion guide → "What to test before you submit" → "Selling subscriptions changes the checklist" (soft link to a RevenueCat guide) → "Store readiness checklist" → FAQ. No pricing anywhere on the page, no author bio. **What to copy**: the per-platform informational branching (a visitor self-identifies their stack and reads only the relevant block) and the pattern of linking out to adjacent guides as the only CTA for most of the page.
2. **[stripe.com/resources/more/how-to-incorporate-your-startup](https://stripe.com/resources/more/how-to-incorporate-your-startup)** (fetched directly, confirmed order) — Introduction → "Benefits of incorporating" → "Challenges to be aware of" → "How to incorporate your startup" → "Where to incorporate" → "What documents do I need" → **"How Stripe Atlas can help"** (the only promotional section, placed last) → footer. ## What we are NOT doing and why

- **Not a short, single-screen landing page** (Julian Shapiro's hero-then-CTA pattern). CXL's own evidence says short-form wins for simple, low-price, high-familiarity offers; this offer is the opposite on all three axes ([cxl.com/blog/long-form-or-short-form](https://cxl.com/blog/long-form-or-short-form/)).
- **Not search-engine-first content**. Google explicitly warns against content "made primarily to gain search engine rankings" rather than to help the reader ([developers.google.com/.../creating-helpful-content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)) — every informational section above exists to answer a real question (and is reused, not invented, from offer.md's already-researched facts), not to stuff a keyword.
- **Not stock photography or decorative illustration**. Covered above under Images — real screenshots and the existing portrait substitute for every image slot.
- **Not treating FAQPage schema as an SEO growth lever**. Its rich-result display was retired by Google sometime in 2026 across multiple corroborating trade sources; keep the markup for semantic/AI-answer value, but don't oversell it internally as a ranking tactic.
- **Not hiding the price behind a "contact us" form**, unlike doola's reference page above. Offer.md's own market research found that no competitor in this market publishes Hebrew, specific prices — that transparency is a stated differentiator and should survive the redesign, just moved to the end of the page rather than removed.
- **Not multiple competing calls to action.** Every CTA on the page, soft or hard, points to the same WhatsApp conversation — never a mix of form, email, and chat links competing for the same decision, consistent with Julian Shapiro's one-dominant-action convention even though the overall page shape rejects his short-form structure ([julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages](https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages)).
