# App Publishing Service: Google Play + App Store

Nov 2, 2026 · @Alex

## The offer

You publish a client's finished app to Google Play and the App Store from your own verified organization accounts, keep it updated while it lives there, and transfer it to the client's own accounts when they are ready. The client skips Google's 12-testers-for-14-days gate, skips waiting weeks for a D-U-N-S number, and gets a Hebrew-speaking human who handles the listing, the compliance forms and the review back-and-forth.

Who it is for: vibe coders, solo founders and small businesses in Israel who built an app (Flutter, React Native, Expo, native) and have no store accounts, no Mac, or no patience for store bureaucracy. Not for: gambling, dating, crypto, adult, loan or medical-claims apps, or anything that collects sensitive data without a clear privacy policy. Those stay off your accounts.

What the service is not: you do not build, fix or test the app, you do not guarantee Apple or Google approval, and you never rent or share account access. Publishing and transfer are services; approval is the store's decision.

## Platform rules and risks

Google supports your model cleanly. Apple does not: publish iOS apps on the client's own Apple account, with you as Admin, and sell that as the service.

**Google Play**

- Org accounts with a verified D-U-N-S are exempt from the 12-testers / 14-days rule; it applies only to personal accounts created after 13 Nov 2023, per app ([Testers Community guide](https://www.testerscommunity.com/guides/how-many-testers-do-you-need-google-play)).
- Transfer is an official process: both accounts fully registered and active, you submit the [transfer form](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/contact/appt) with both accounts' IDs and $25 registration transaction IDs; support replies within 2 business days. Reviews, ratings and installs carry over. Pre-transfer orders stay tied to your account, so keep it alive for refunds.
- Paid apps or in-app purchases: the target account needs an active payments profile, and an app with in-app purchases is unpublished until the client republishes it after transfer.
- Risk: every policy strike lands on your account. Google also suspends accounts "associated" with banned ones. Vet apps, sign a removal right, and never share login.
- Transfer target: production access is granted per account, not per app (\[Play Console Help\](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)), and Google Play Product Experts report that moving an app does not clear a testing obligation. So offer transfer only into an organization account (D-U-N-S) or a personal account that already has production access. Say this on the site.

**App Store**

- Apple's [transfer criteria](https://www.developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/app-transfer-criteria): the app must have at least one version released to the App Store, no pending review or pre-order, both accounts active with the latest agreements accepted. Transfer runs inside App Store Connect by the Account Holder and takes up to two business days; the recipient has 60 days to accept.
- Guideline 5.2.1: apps should be submitted by the person or entity that owns the IP. Guideline 4.2.6 rejects apps from a template or generation service unless the content provider submits them directly ([GoodBarber on 4.2.6 and 5.2.1](https://www.goodbarber.com/help/manage-developer-accounts-apple-google-r97/understand-apple-developer-account-compliance-with-guidelines-4-2-6-and-5-2-1-a323/)). Agencies that published many client apps under one account got rejected and told to move them to client accounts.
- Guideline 4.3 (spam): many unrelated apps under one developer account is a known trigger. A publishing service by definition accumulates unrelated apps.
- Conclusion: on Apple, the pain you solve is not the account, it is the Mac, Xcode, certificates, provisioning, TestFlight, privacy labels and review rejections. The client pays Apple $99/year for their own account (individual, no D-U-N-S needed) and invites you as Admin. This is exactly what Fiverr Pro sellers do and what Apple wants.
- Exception you can keep quiet and judge case by case: a short pre-launch on your account for a client who is mid-registration, with transfer right after first release. Do not advertise it.

**Both stores**

- You never guarantee approval. Fiverr sellers state this explicitly; copy it.
- New Apple builds must use Xcode 26 / iOS 26 SDK since 28 Apr 2026, and from Sep 2026 the updated age-rating questionnaire is required. Expect vibe-coded projects to fail here first; price the fix separately.

## Client intake checklist

This is your website's intake form. Nothing starts until every required item is in.

**Business and contact**

- [ ] Full name, business name, ID or company number (for the agreement)
- [ ] Email and WhatsApp
- [ ] Support email and website URL to show on the store listing
- [ ] Signed agreement (see below) and payment

**App basics (both stores)**

- [ ] App name (max 30 characters on both stores) and subtitle / short description (Google 80, Apple 30)
- [ ] Full description, Hebrew and English
- [ ] Category, and whether it is free, paid, or has in-app purchases or subscriptions
- [ ] Privacy policy URL, live and public, matching what the app collects
- [ ] Terms of use URL if there are subscriptions (Apple requires it)
- [ ] App icon 1024 x 1024 PNG, no transparency, no rounded corners
- [ ] Screenshots: Google phone 1080 x 1920 or similar, at least 2; Apple 6.7 inch iPhone 1290 x 2796, at least 3; iPad if the app runs on iPad
- [ ] Feature graphic 1024 x 500 (Google only)
- [ ] Demo account (username + password) if the app has login; reviewers must be able to get in
- [ ] Answers to the Data Safety (Google) and App Privacy (Apple) questionnaires: what data is collected, is it shared, is it encrypted, can users delete it
- [ ] Age rating answers, including Apple's 2026 social-media capability questions
- [ ] Does the app use ads, location, camera, contacts, health data, user-generated content, or any SDK that tracks users (Facebook, Firebase Analytics, AdMob)
- [ ] Account deletion: if users can sign up, there must be an in-app way to delete the account (both stores require it)

**Google Play specific**

- [ ] Signed release AAB (not APK) targeting Android 16 (API level 36) or higher, required for all new apps and updates since 31 Aug 2026 (\[Play policy\](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11926878?hl=en-419))
- [ ] The upload keystore stays with the client; you enroll the app in Play App Signing
- [ ] Package name (cannot change later)
- [ ] Confirmation whether the client already has any Google Play account (to avoid duplicate-account issues)

**App Store specific**

- [ ] Client's Apple Developer account active ($99/year paid), latest agreements accepted by the Account Holder, tax and banking filled if the app sells anything, and you invited as Admin (App Manager at minimum) under Users and Access in App Store Connect. Never their password, only the invite
- [ ] Source project (Xcode, Flutter, React Native, Expo) in a repo you can clone, or a signed .ipa if they build themselves
- [ ] Bundle ID (cannot change later)
- [ ] Any third-party keys needed to build (Firebase plist, API keys) or confirmation they are already in the repo
- [ ] Apple age rating and export compliance answer (does it use encryption beyond HTTPS)
- [ ] Review notes: a short explanation of what the app does and how to test it

**Compliance gate (you check before accepting)**

- [ ] Not in a banned category (gambling, adult, crypto trading, loans, medical claims, copycat of a known brand)
- [ ] App opens, logs in and performs its core function on a real device without crashing
- [ ] Privacy policy exists and matches the data collected
- [ ] No copyrighted assets or brand names the client does not own

## Process

From first message to transfer, a Google-only client takes about one week plus however long they stay; adding Apple adds 3 to 7 days for the first review.

1. Inquiry: the client fills the intake form on the website (checklist above). You reply within one business day with a yes, a no, or questions.
2. Compliance check: you install the build on a real device, read the privacy policy, check the category. 15 minutes. Decline anything that would put your Google account at risk.
3. Agreement and payment: client signs the agreement (digital signature is fine), pays the one-time fee plus the first month if on a plan. No work before payment.
4. Google publish: create the app on your org console, upload the AAB, enroll Play App Signing, fill listing, Data Safety, content rating, target audience, ads declaration, set countries, submit to production. Typical review: 1 to 7 days.
5. Apple publish: client's Apple account is active and you are Admin. Create the App Store Connect record with the client's Bundle ID, set certificates and provisioning, build and archive on your Mac, upload, TestFlight sanity check, fill listing, privacy labels, age rating, review notes and demo account, submit. Typical review: 1 to 3 days, rejections common on first submit.
6. Review loop: you forward every rejection to the client with a plain-Hebrew explanation of what to change. Fixing the app is their job (or their developer's). Resubmitting is yours, within the plan limits.
7. Live: you send the store links, and on Google you add the client as a viewer-level user on that app if they want console stats (optional, read-only).
8. Updates: client sends a new AAB / new build through a simple form or WhatsApp; you upload, update release notes, submit. Up to the plan limit; extra updates billed.
9. Transfer (Google): client opens their own account, preferably an organization with D-U-N-S. They send you their developer account ID and $25 transaction ID. You submit the transfer form. Google replies within 2 business days. You confirm the app is gone from your console and send a handover note.
10. Close: final invoice, plan cancelled, keystore reminder, and a request for a review or referral.

## Agreement terms

A one-to-two page Hebrew agreement, signed digitally before any upload. These are the clauses; have a lawyer check the wording once, then reuse it.

1. Ownership: the app, its code, name, brand and content belong to the client. You hold only a publishing license for the duration of the service. You never claim rights in the app.
2. Scope: you publish, maintain the listing, upload updates the client supplies, and handle store correspondence. You do not develop, fix, test or guarantee approval or ranking.
3. Client warranties: the client owns or licenses everything in the app, the app does not break any law or store policy, the privacy policy is accurate, and the client is responsible for user data and for what the app does.
4. Removal right: you may unpublish or remove the app at any time, with notice where possible, if it receives a policy strike, a legal complaint, or you judge it endangers your developer account. Fees for the current month are not refunded in that case.
5. Account independence: no login sharing in either direction. On Apple, the client's own account is used; on Google, the app sits on your account and the client gets read-only access on request.
6. Transfer: on Google, transfer on written request once the client's account is eligible, within 14 days, subject to Google's rules; on Apple nothing to transfer because the app is already on the client's account. Transfer fee as per the price list. If Google refuses the transfer, you keep hosting under the plan or the client may ask you to unpublish; no refund of past fees.
7. Keys and signing: the client keeps the Android upload keystore and all source. You keep copies only as needed to publish and delete them 30 days after transfer or termination.
8. Data: pre-transfer purchase records stay with your Google account by Google's design; you will assist with refunds for 12 months after transfer. Crash and analytics data in the console is shared on request.
9. Payment: one-time fees in advance; plans monthly in advance; unpaid plan after 14 days means the app may be unpublished until paid. Prices exclude store registration fees.
10. Termination: either side with 30 days notice. On termination the client chooses transfer (if eligible) or unpublish. Apps cannot stay on your account without an active plan.
11. Liability: capped at the fees paid in the last 3 months. No liability for store decisions, downtime, lost revenue, or the app's own behaviour.
12. Confidentiality: you keep the client's source, keys and business details private.
13. Governing law: Israel, courts of Tel Aviv.
