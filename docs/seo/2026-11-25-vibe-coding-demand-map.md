# Vibe-coding demand map — 2026-11-25

Research for the 10-guide programme Alex authorised 24 Nov: audience widens
from business owners who hire him to the Hebrew vibe-coding crowd itself
(the 52,400-member "Vibe Coding - Israel" Facebook group and its kind),
inside which business owners building for themselves also sit. He wants to
be read as the professional in that room, for both a hobbyist builder and a
business owner.

Read-only. 21 live Google.com searches, Hebrew UI (`hl=he&gl=il`), Alex's own
Brave profile with the Claude extension — the only browser used, nothing
installed, no sign-in. **All reads done 2026-11-25.** Grounded first in the
site's own `docs/seo/fb-group-demand-2026-11.md` (the 2026-11-04 scan of the
same Facebook group) so every candidate traces to a real post, not a guess —
cited inline as "(FB: ...)" where that is the source. Reused rather than
repeated: the ten queries already in `docs/seo/content-programme-2026-11.md`
and their verdicts (app-or-website, freelancer-or-agency, off-the-ai-builder,
do-you-need-a-mac, play-testers-requirement, apple-rejection-copycat,
who-owns-the-developer-account, app-publishing-costs, whatsapp-ai-cloud-api,
construction) — none of those is repeated here.

**The one caution this carries from yesterday's console work
(`2026-11-24-console-capabilities-and-growth-evidence.md`): a live read from
a signed-in browser is never trusted alone.** None of the findings below
claim a ranking position for handinhandai.com itself (it has no page on any
of these subjects yet, so there is nothing to misread) — the trap does not
apply the same way here. What it does mean: "who ranks" below is a snapshot
of one read, one day, and this property's own prior research
(`keyword-briefs-2026-11.md`) already proved some of these pages are not
reproducible between sessions. Treat the CROWDED calls as directionally
solid (several independent, dated, well-matched competitors is a strong
signal regardless of exact order) and the few OPEN calls as provisional
until re-read closer to drafting.

## The meta-finding, stated before the list

**This territory is far more crowded than the app-publishing niche the
first ten articles were planned against.** That programme's 2026-11-04
research found most of its queries open or only lightly contested. Here,
18 of 21 tested subjects already have multiple dated, competent, Hebrew
written guides — a prolific, repeat-name cottage industry of Israeli AI
consultants and bloggers (recurring across unrelated queries: **elya-studio.com**
across three different subdomains — main, `vibe.`, `strategy.` — **יהב
רובין** (yahavrubin.com), **Let's AI**, **tzedek.me**, **עמית טק**
(amittech.dev), **Skills IL** (agentskills.co.il), **smartup.co.il**,
**spiritt.ai**, **מור ממן** (mmn.co.il), plus Geektime and mainstream news
(YNET, mako, Calcalist) on the security angle specifically). Publishing into
this space on the generic, most-searched version of a subject is very
unlikely to rank quickly. The openings that exist are narrower and newer
than the generic definitional or comparison queries.

## The three groupings asked for

**Phrases where a written Hebrew guide would plausibly rank quickly:**
- **#19, selling/monetising an app you built with AI.** The one real
  opening found. No dedicated competitor owns the Hebrew "I built this,
  now how do I make it a real paying product" angle; the generic AI
  Overview answer (SaaS vs. selling the project outright, specific tool
  names) gives a ready structure to go deeper than. It is also the exact
  audience crossover Alex wants: the FB group's own stuck-with-customers
  posts are this reader.
- **#10, the felt pain of a growing AI-built codebase, correctly phrased**
  — provisional, not confirmed open the way #19 is. The generic technical
  framing ("architecture") is contested; the narrative, how-a-hobbyist-
  actually-describes-it framing was never found on Google at all (see #9),
  which could mean either an opening or no searchable door — needs a
  second, differently-worded read before committing an article to it.

**Phrases too crowded to be worth it (as the bare, generic query):**
#1 security generally, #2 RLS specifically, #3 the hacked-app/incident
framing, #4 API-key basics, #5 Lovable/Base44 cost, #6 vibe-coding
definition, #7 Claude Code vs. Cursor, #8 Git/GitHub basics, #11 ToS/privacy
cost, #12 Israeli payment processing, #14 AI-agent definition, #15/#16
Claude Skills definition and installation, #17 prompt-writing, #18 cheap API
providers, #20 no-code vs. vibe-coding, #21 learning alone. Sixteen of
twenty-one. Any of these could still be written as a narrower long-tail
angle a generalist competitor hasn't taken (the pattern that worked for
`who-owns-the-developer-account` in the first programme), but none is open
on the subject as stated.

**Phrases with real demand that only video or social currently serves:**
- **#9, the literal "my app got tangled" pain** — this is the clearest
  case. Real, the highest-engagement post found in the entire Facebook
  scan (37 reactions, 88 comments), and it simply does not exist as a
  Google query; Google reads the Hebrew phrasing as a phone problem.
  The content that *does* exist for this exact feeling lives on Instagram/
  TikTok and in Facebook comment threads (short clips, "despair" posts),
  not as written guides. If this subject is worth having, it is reached by
  participating in that community directly (comments, a post of his own),
  not by SEO — the `fb-group-post`/`fb-radar` skills' territory, not this
  one.
- **#13, a Green Invoice clone** — same shape: real, high-engagement
  community demand (17 reactions, 83 comments) with zero matching Google
  search volume. Unlike #9, this one is not even search-intent-adjacent —
  Google treats it as noise rather than misreading it — so there is likely
  no written-guide version of this subject worth building at all, social
  or otherwise; it reads as a one-off curiosity inside the group rather
  than a recurring need.

## Flagged: where the honest guide argues against hiring him

- **#11, terms of use / privacy policy.** The honest, correct answer for
  a reader is "pay a lawyer ~₪1,500-2,000 plus VAT, or use one of these
  named legal-tech services" — not a development service. Still worth
  writing for the trust and completeness it buys (the FB group asks this
  constantly), but it should not promise a conversion into his own pipeline
  the way the app-publishing guides do.
- **#12, Israeli payment processing.** Softer version of the same thing —
  the correct first answer is "pick Tranzila/Cardcom/iCredit/Rapyd
  directly," though *wiring one of them into an AI-built app* is a real
  service he can still sell, so this one argues against hiring him for the
  provider choice while still supporting hiring him for the integration.
- **#21, learning vibe coding alone.** Not a legal or financial
  argument but a positioning one: the reader explicitly wants to not need
  anyone. A guide here is goodwill/authority content at best, not a lead
  generator — worth knowing before spending one of the ten slots on it.

## What could not be established

- Whether #10's real, narrative framing ("my prototype became unmanageable
  and I didn't know what to do") has **any** matching Google phrasing at
  all — only the generic technical term was tested and found contested.
  Needs 2-3 more phrasing attempts before ruling it open or closed.
- Whether #14's exact FB framing ("is an AI agent physically a website, an
  app, or software on a computer") has independent search volume distinct
  from the generic "what is an AI agent" business-consulting query — not
  tested separately; likely shares #9's and #13's fate (real confusion, no
  matching phrase) but unconfirmed.
- Exact ranking positions and AI Overview stability for every subject here
  are single-day, single-session reads, per the caution above — not
  re-tested a second time the way the app-publishing programme's queries
  were in its own research.
