# Facebook group demand scan — Vibe Coding Israel (2026-11-04)

## Group identified

- **Name:** Vibe Coding - Israel
- **URL:** https://www.facebook.com/groups/1684554685829832
- **Type:** Public group
- **Members:** 52.4K
- **Language:** Hebrew (dominant), with English tool/product names mixed in
- **About (as written in the group):** "ברוכים הבאים ל-Vibe Coding Israel. הבית של היוצרים והבונים בישראל שהופכים רעיונות למוצרים עובדים עם AI - ומשתפים איך עשו את זה." — "Welcome to Vibe Coding Israel. Home for creators and builders in Israel who turn ideas into working products with AI, and share how they did it."

**Is it a buyers' group or a sellers' group?** Neither, cleanly. It is a **builders' community**: individual, mostly non-professional people building their own apps/sites with AI tools (Lovable, Base44, Claude Code, Cursor, Bolt) and posting progress, questions, and brag-posts. Inside that community two sub-populations show up repeatedly and matter for Hand in Hand AI:
1. **Solo builders who hit a wall** their AI tool can't get them past (store submission, legal compliance, security review, Meta/WhatsApp verification, migrating off a no-code tool) — these are warm leads for a done-for-you service.
2. **Small business owners / people with an idea** posting freelance-hire requests or "looking for a technical partner" asks inside the same feed — classic company-need / partnership posts.

## Method note

Used Facebook's in-group search (per the `fb-radar` skill's search-first convention) over themed Hebrew/English phrases, newest-first where the UI allowed it, rather than scrolling the full feed. This found genuine question/need/complaint posts efficiently but Facebook's search result view does not expose reliable absolute timestamps or resolvable permalinks without clicking into each post individually (consistent with what the `fb-radar` skill already documented: links are obfuscated until clicked). Given this is a one-off research pass (not a commenting run), permalinks were not resolved — each item below is cited by author name and verbatim quote, which is enough to re-find the post via in-group search if needed. Comment/reaction counts are given as an engagement proxy for recency and relevance; roughly 30 distinct relevant posts were collected across searches (plus brag/show-off posts used only as theme-confirmation context, not counted as "asks").
